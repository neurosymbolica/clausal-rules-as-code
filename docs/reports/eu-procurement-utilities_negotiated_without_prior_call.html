unsuitability_determination_by_reference_to_arts_78_and_80 AUTHORIZED

reason
Art 50(a) defines an unsuitable REQUEST by reference to exclusion under Arts 78(1)/80(1) or failure of the selection criteria laid down under Arts 78 or 80. Those are sibling determinations; here unsuitability is the atomic input the definition describes.
consequence
A caller must establish unsuitability itself, under Arts 78/80. This domain takes the finding as given and will report `use_lawful` on an (a) plea whose unsuitability claim would not survive those articles.

proviso_attaches_to_points_ii_and_iii_only EXPANSION

NOT YET REVIEWED — no qualified person has signed off this reading.

question
The Art 50(c) proviso reads "The exceptions set out in points (ii) and (iii) shall only apply when no reasonable alternative or substitute exists and the absence of competition is not the result of an artificial narrowing down of the parameters of the procurement". Does it also condition point (i), a unique work of art or artistic performance?
chosen
points_ii_and_iii_only
rationale
The sentence names points (ii) and (iii) and no others. This is the clearest textual signal in the article, and Directive 2014/24 Art 32(2)(b) attaches its identical proviso the same way -- one of the genuine points of continuity between the two instruments.
authority
none

Readings

  • points_ii_and_iii_only — TAKEN

    A unique work of art is a complete ground on `only_particular_ operator_can_perform` alone. The proviso's own words name two points and the drafter listed them, so naming a third would be re-drafting.

  • all_three_sub_points — not taken more conservative

    Every sole-operator plea would additionally need no reasonable alternative and no artificial narrowing. Stricter, and it would catch an entity commissioning a bespoke artwork to escape competition -- but it reads a restriction into a sentence that enumerates its own targets.

contract_type_words_are_jurisdictional_limits EXPANSION

NOT YET REVIEWED — no qualified person has signed off this reading.

question
Four grounds open with a contract type -- "in the case of supply contracts" (e), "for supplies" (g) and (h), "where the service contract concerned" (j). Are those words a LIMIT on the ground's availability, or a description of the usual case?
chosen
limits
rationale
The article names a contract type in exactly four of ten grounds. A reading on which that choice changes nothing cannot explain why the drafter made it four times and not ten or zero. The permissive reading is also the one that expands a derogation, which cuts against the settled rule that derogations from the obligation to compete are strictly interpreted.
authority
none

Readings

  • limits — TAKEN

    A ground pleaded outside its type is unavailable however well its other conditions are made out, so contract_type_key is decisive on four of the ten grounds.

  • descriptive — not taken

    The type words would constrain nothing and the four grounds would be available for any contract. More permissive -- it would let an entity plead the bargain-purchase ground for a works contract -- and it would make the drafter's decision to name a type in four grounds and not in the other six meaningless.

the_pleaded_ground_governs EXPANSION

NOT YET REVIEWED — no qualified person has signed off this reading.

question
If the entity pleads ground X and X is not made out, but the conditions of some other ground Y happen to be satisfied on the same facts, is the use lawful?
chosen
plea_governs
rationale
The justification an entity files names a ground, and review of it is review of THAT ground; the burden of establishing a derogation lies on the party relying on it. Treating any latent ground as a save would also mean an entity that pleaded nothing coherent could be rescued by a ground it never invoked and may not have evidenced. RECORDED AS CONTESTABLE because an administrative court applying the objective-lawfulness of the award, rather than reviewing the stated reasons, might well take the other view.
authority
none

Readings

  • plea_governs — TAKEN

    `use_unlawful`. The verdict answers the ground actually relied on, and the interface returns `n_a` for GROUND. A reviewer can always re-run the domain pleading Y.

  • any_available_ground_saves_it — not taken

    `use_lawful` on Y. More permissive, and it would make the profile's pleaded_ground_key inert -- the domain would answer "is ANY ground made out", which is a different question from the one an award notice's justification poses.

the_unsuitability_sentences_are_definitions_not_limbs EXPANSION

NOT YET REVIEWED — no qualified person has signed off this reading.

question
Art 50(a) is followed by two sentences defining when a tender, and when a request to participate, is "not suitable". Are those additional cumulative conditions of the ground, or definitions of the finding the ground already requires?
chosen
definitions
rationale
The sentences are drafted as definitions ("shall be considered not to be suitable where...") and they define a term the ground uses, rather than adding a requirement to it. Treating them as limbs would import the Arts 78/80 determinations this domain defers, which would make the ground undecidable on the facts the profile carries.
authority
none

Readings

  • definitions — TAKEN

    The ground has two conditions -- unsuitability and unaltered initial conditions -- and the definitions fix what the first one MEANS. The profile carries one finding, `no_suitable_tenders_or_requests_received`.

  • additional_limbs — not taken more conservative

    The profile would carry separate findings for irrelevance, manifest incapability, exclusion under Arts 78(1)/80(1) and failure of selection criteria, each independently required. Stricter, and it would make the ground harder to establish -- but it would also require this domain to decide Arts 78/80 questions it explicitly defers.
