## Scope

Swiss law in force (a reading, not a lawyer's). The breach-reporting duties of the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (DSG, SR 235.1, German text, fedlex consolidation Stand 7 July 2025, authoritative; English glosses unofficial) Art 24: the controller's report to the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (EDOeB) under Art 24(1), the processor's report to the controller under Art 24(3), and the information of the data subject under Art 24(4) with its restriction, deferral or omission under Art 24(5), with the definition of a breach of data security in DSG Art 5 letter h. For comparison its own limb for Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR) Art 33(1), the controller's notification to the supervisory authority, compared on the same facts with the Swiss authority duty (the two threshold provisions make any difference). Every open-textured matter (breach, likely high risk, necessity, the Art 24(5) grounds, the EU breach and unlikely-risk judgements) is a tri-state judgement of the caller; an unsure one that decides goes to a lawyer as needs_legal_review. ## Provisions deferred, and what follows

??? info "swiss_report_content"

    **Why:** DSG Art 24(2) and DSV Art 15(1) list what a report to the Commissioner must at least contain (the kind of breach, its consequences, the measures, and in the DSV the time and duration, the categories and numbers, and a contact person, several 'soweit möglich'); the order does not check a report's content (reading (3)).

    **Consequence for a user:** No answer says whether a report contains what it must.

??? info "swiss_report_in_phases"

    **Why:** DSV Art 15(2) lets the controller supply missing items later, as quickly as possible, when it cannot report them all at once; the order does not check a report's content or timing (readings (3) and (4)).

    **Consequence for a user:** No answer says whether items supplied later were supplied in time.

??? info "swiss_information_content"

    **Why:** DSV Art 15(3) sets what the information to the data subject must at least contain, in simple and understandable language; the order says only whether the information is owed (reading (5)).

    **Consequence for a user:** No answer says whether an information contains what it must.

??? info "swiss_documentation_duty"

    **Why:** DSV Art 15(4) requires the controller to document breaches and keep the documentation for at least two years from the report; it is a duty this order does not answer.

    **Consequence for a user:** No answer says what must be documented or for how long.

??? info "swiss_data_security_duty"

    **Why:** DSG Art 8 sets the duty of data security and the Federal Council's power to set minimum requirements; pinned because Art 61(c) refers to Art 8(3) (reading (9)); the order does not answer whether security was adequate.

    **Consequence for a user:** No answer says whether the measures were adequate.

??? info "swiss_penalties"

    **Why:** DSG Arts 60 and 61 set fines for private persons for breaches of other listed duties; neither names Art 24 (reading (9)); no query answers on penalties.

    **Consequence for a user:** No answer says whether a fine can be imposed.
