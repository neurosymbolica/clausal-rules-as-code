## Scope

Whether ONE incident of ONE relevant entity of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690 (DNS service providers, TLD name registries, cloud computing, data centre, content delivery network, managed and managed security service providers, providers of online marketplaces, online search engines and social networking services platforms, trust service providers) is a significant incident for the purposes of Article 23(3) of Directive (EU) 2022/2555: Art 3(1) points (a) to (g) as alternative criteria (the money test, the four judgements, recurring incidents under Art 4, and the entity-type criteria of Arts 5 to 14), with Art 3(2) taking scheduled interruptions and planned maintenance consequences out of every criterion. Results: significant, not_significant, or a status (indeterminate naming one missing key, needs_legal_review for a decisive unsure judgement, input_problems). ## Provisions deferred, and what follows

??? info "technical_and_methodological_requirements"

    **Why:** Art 2 points to the Annex for the technical and methodological requirements of the cybersecurity risk-management measures of Directive (EU) 2022/2555 Art 21(2), requires a level of security appropriate to the risks, and requires the entity to document why it considers a requirement 'where appropriate', 'where applicable' or 'to the extent feasible' not appropriate, not applicable or not feasible. It does not bear on whether an incident is significant.

    **Consequence for a user:** The answers never say which security measures an entity must take or document.

??? info "counting_users_impacted"

    **Why:** Art 3(3) tells the entity what to consider when it calculates the number of users impacted for Arts 7 and 9 to 14 (customers with a contract granting access; persons associated with business customers). The order takes the counts as the caller's, made that way (reading (9)).

    **Consequence for a user:** The user figures are the caller's; an answer is only as good as the count.
