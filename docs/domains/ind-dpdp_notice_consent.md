## Scope

Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 ss 2(g), 2(i), 4, 5, 6 and 7, with the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 rules 1(4), 3, 4 and 5 and the First and Second Schedules. Decided here, one question per query: whether personal data may be processed on the ground of consent (s 4(1)(a), s 4(2), s 6(1), s 6(4)); whether it may be processed for a listed legitimate use (s 4(1)(b), s 7, one clause per question); which notice the Data Fiduciary owes (s 5(1), s 5(2)(a), s 5(3), with rule 3 from 13 May 2027 on the calculated date of rule 1(4)); what a withdrawal of consent requires of the Data Fiduciary (s 6(4) to (6)); whether a person may act as a Consent Manager (s 2(g), s 6(7) to (9), rule 4(2)(a), (3) and (5)(a)); and the fixed lists of notice contents, request requirements, legitimate uses, Second Schedule standards and Consent Manager conditions and obligations. ## Provisions deferred, and what follows

??? info "lead_in_words_of_listed_items"

    **Why:** These units hold the lead-in words of the items listed under them (rule 3(c): the communication link and other means; First Schedule Part A item 9: independent certification; Part B items 3, 4 and 11: the record, access to it and publication); each item is a content_item of provision_lists with its own citation, and its NAME atom carries the lead-in sense. The lead-in words are substantive (for example the duty to maintain a record, to publish, to be independently certified) but have no limb of their own beyond the items.

    **Consequence for a user:** None beyond the items: the items they introduce are listed with their own citations; no query checks a record, a publication or a certification.

??? info "substantive_lead_in_words"

    **Why:** First Schedule Part B item 12 (effective audit mechanisms, with the outcome reported to the Board periodically and on such other occasions as the Board may direct) and the Second Schedule lead-in (its heading and the duty to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures, in accordance with applicable law, to ensure effective observance of the standards listed) carry words of their own beyond their items; their items are content_items of provision_lists (audit_... and the eleven standards), but the lead-in duties have no item and no limb.

    **Consequence for a user:** The audit items do not say that the outcome is reported to the Board periodically and when the Board directs; the Second Schedule items do not say that technical and organisational measures are to be implemented. No query decides either.

??? info "definitions_read_inside_judgements_and_lists"

    **Why:** Quoted for the readings: 'certain legitimate uses' (s 2(d), reading (7)); Data Principal with a child's parents or lawful guardian and a person with disability's lawful guardian (s 2(j)); Data Processor (s 2(k), reading (4)); processing (s 2(x)); specified purpose (s 2(za), read inside consent_meets_section_6_1 and legitimate_use_conditions_met). No limb decides any of them.

    **Consequence for a user:** The answers never say who the Data Principal is for a child or a person with disability, or what the specified purpose is.

??? info "commencement_of_rule_4"

    **Why:** Rule 1(3) dates rule 4 (13 November 2026 on the calculated date of reading (3)); consent_manager_status takes the registration as a caller fact and reads no date (Q11).

    **Consequence for a user:** An answer never says whether rule 4 was in force on a given day.

??? info "consent_before_commencement_may_continue"

    **Why:** s 5(2)(b) lets processing on a consent given before commencement continue until she withdraws it; the order reads such a consent as consent_given (reading (6), Q8); no limb cites it.

    **Consequence for a user:** consent_ground treats the earlier consent like any other; it never cites s 5(2)(b).

??? info "part_invalid_consent"

    **Why:** s 6(2) makes the infringing part of a consent invalid to that extent; the order does not split a consent (Q7).

    **Consequence for a user:** A caller with a partly invalid consent asks per purpose; no answer says which part is invalid.

??? info "branches_and_explanation_of_section_7"

    **Why:** The two branches of s 7(b) and the Explanation to s 7(h) are read inside the judgement legitimate_use_conditions_met (reading (7), Q17).

    **Consequence for a user:** The answers never say which branch of s 7(b) applied or whether an event was a disaster.

??? info "state_use_rule_5"

    **Why:** Rule 5(1) makes processing under s 7(b) follow the Second Schedule standards, which provision_lists lists; rule 5(2) explains three phrases of that rule. Whether processing follows the standards is not read (reading (8), Q10).

    **Consequence for a user:** legitimate_use_ground for clause (b) never says whether the standards were followed.
