## Scope

Directive (EU) 2022/2555 (NIS 2), Arts 32(2), 32(4), 32(5), 33(2), 33(4), 34(4), 34(5) and 35(1) to (3): for an essential or important entity, (A) the least national maximum fine a Member State must provide for an infringement of Article 21 or 23 (a floor of the national ceiling, never a fine; no Union minimum for any other provision); (B) the minimum supervisory, enforcement and escalation powers a Member State must give its competent authorities, in article order, without the Art 32(5) powers for a public administration entity; (C) whether the Art 32(5) escalation powers are exercisable (four conditions; the eligibility verdict of the facade, with a cited checklist); (D) whether a GDPR Art 58(2)(i) fine for the same conduct bars the NIS2 fine (Art 35(2)); (E) the duty to inform the data protection supervisory authorities (Art 35(1), (3)). Open-textured judgements (ineffective earlier measures, a possible notifiable personal data breach, the same conduct) are handed to the user or a lawyer, never decided by a crisp test. Out of scope: the actual national maximum and penalties (Art 36, country orders), the choice of measure or fine amount (Art 32(7) factors), entity classification ...

## Provisions deferred, and what follows

??? info "general_principles_of_supervision_and_fines"

    **Why:** Art 32(1), 33(1) and 34(1) say supervisory and enforcement measures and fines must be effective, proportionate and dissuasive, taking account of the circumstances of each case; Art 33(1) says the authorities act on evidence of non-compliance by an important entity "where necessary"; Art 34(2) says fines are imposed in addition to the listed measures. These are open-textured principles of exercise and no limb of this order reads them.

    **Consequence for a user:** The answers do not say whether a measure or a fine is proportionate or whether an authority must act; the caller judges that itself.

??? info "how_powers_are_exercised"

    **Why:** Art 32(3) and 33(3) require the authority to state the purpose of an information request and specify the information requested; Art 32(8) requires detailed reasoning, notice of preliminary findings and a reasonable time for observations. They bind the authority's procedure, not the powers it must have.

    **Consequence for a user:** The answers do not say whether an authority followed the procedure; the caller checks it itself.

??? info "factors_for_measures_and_fines"

    **Why:** Art 32(7) lists the factors an authority takes due account of in choosing a measure (seriousness, duration, previous infringements, damage, intent or negligence, mitigation, codes of conduct, cooperation); Art 33(5) applies Art 32(6) to (8) to important entities and Art 34(3) applies the Art 32(7) factors to the amount of a fine. The Directive gives the factors and no weights, so this order does not compute a measure or a fine.

    **Consequence for a user:** The answers never say which measure or what fine an authority should choose; they give only the floors, the minimum powers and the conditions of this order.

??? info "liability_of_natural_persons"

    **Why:** Art 32(6) (natural persons who represent or control an essential entity can be held liable for breach of their duties to ensure compliance) concerns persons, not the entity, and is not a limb of any query here.

    **Consequence for a user:** The answers do not say whether a natural person can be held liable.

??? info "periodic_penalties_and_public_administration_and_courts"

    **Why:** Art 34(6) (Member States MAY provide periodic penalty payments), Art 34(7) (Member States may decide whether and how far fines apply to public administration entities) and Art 34(8) (fines imposed by courts where the legal system has no administrative fines) leave the rule to national law; they set no Union minimum.

    **Consequence for a user:** The answers apply the Art 34 floors to every entity; whether national law fines a public administration entity, and how, is for the country orders.

??? info "supervisory_power_lead_ins_and_targeted_audit_rules"

    **Why:** Art 32(2) and 33(2) open the list of supervisory powers with a lead-in, whose points minimum_powers answers and whose words at least reading (4) reads, and close it with rules on targeted security audits - they rest on risk assessments by the authority or the audited entity or on other risk-related information, their results are made available to the competent authority, and the costs of an audit by an independent body are paid by the audited entity unless the authority decides otherwise in duly substantiated cases.

    **Consequence for a user:** The answers do not say on what basis an audit is carried out, who receives its results or who pays for it; the caller reads the Directive text for that.
