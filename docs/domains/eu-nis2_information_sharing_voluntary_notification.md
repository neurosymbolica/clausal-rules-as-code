## Scope

Directive (EU) 2022/2555 (NIS 2) Arts 29(1), 29(2), 29(4) and 30(1): what the Directive requires a Member State to ensure (national law says what an entity must do in fact). Three questions are decided: whether a voluntary notification of an incident, significant incident, cyber threat or near miss can be submitted under Art 30(1) (point (a) essential and important entities, any event; point (b) every other entity, significant incidents, cyber threats and near misses only); whether a voluntary exchange of cybersecurity information is one the Member States must let entities carry out under Art 29(1) (relevant information, and point (a) or point (b) read as alternatives); and whether Art 29(4) requires an essential or important entity to notify the competent authorities on entering, or on the withdrawal from, an information-sharing arrangement of Art 29(2). The open-textured judgements (relevance, the aims of points (a) and (b), whether an arrangement is one of Art 29(2)) are the caller's: an unsure judgement that decides is referred as needs_legal_review. ## Provisions deferred, and what follows

??? info "processing_of_voluntary_notifications_and_no_additional_obligations"

    **Why:** Art 30(2) requires the Member States to process voluntary notifications under the Art 23 procedure, lets them prioritise mandatory notifications, requires the CSIRTs to inform the single points of contact and says that voluntary reporting imposes no additional obligation on the notifying entity. The processing and the priority are authority-side; the last sentence is a constant answer (none) that this order does not ask as a query.

    **Consequence for a user:** The answers never say how a voluntary notification is processed; a voluntary notification adds no obligation to the entity (reading (6)).
