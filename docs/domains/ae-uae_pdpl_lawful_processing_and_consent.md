## Scope

UAE Federal Decree by Law No. (45) of 2021 Concerning the Protection of Personal Data (PDPL), English translation from uaelegislation.gov.ae (the Arabic text prevails and was not read). For ONE processing of personal data on ONE processing date, three questions are decided: (a) pdpl_processing_lawful_basis - whether Art 4 prohibits the processing (allowed_with_consent when the consent meets the Art 1 definition of Consent and Art 6(1) and is not withdrawn under Art 6(2); else allowed_without_consent naming the first of the ten Art 4 cases that holds; else not_allowed); (b) pdpl_consent_valid - whether the consent is valid, naming the first unmet of six conditions; (c) pdpl_processing_controls - whether the Art 5 controls of items 1 to 7 are met, naming the first one failed. Every query gives not_in_force before 2 January 2022 (Art 31). Open-textured judgements (necessity, the cases, the controls, the quality of consent) are the caller's, and an unsure one is referred to a lawyer. Out of scope: Art 4 item 11 and Art 5 item 8 (Executive Regulations, not known to be issued: named gaps), Art 29 regularisation, Arts 2 and 3 (scope and Bureau exemptions), data subject rights, controller ...

??? info "further_controls_in_the_executive_regulations"

    **Why:** Art 5 item 8 adds '[quotation omitted]'. The Executive Regulations are not known to have been issued: a NAMED GAP, see the Executive Regulations (reading (12), Q10).

    **Consequence for a user:** meets_listed_controls never says that the controls of the Executive Regulations are met.

??? info "scope_and_bureau_exemption"

    **Why:** Art 2 (the scope of the Decree-Law and the cases it does not apply to) and Art 3 (the Bureau's power to exempt [quotation omitted] [quotation omitted], by standards of the Executive Regulations) are read by the sibling order on scope and exclusions, not here (reading (2), Q12).

    **Consequence for a user:** Every answer assumes that the Decree-Law applies to the processing and that no Bureau exemption covers it.

??? info "executive_regulations_and_regularisation"

    **Why:** Art 28 (the Executive Regulations, due within six months of promulgation) and Art 29 (regularisation within six months of the issue of the Executive Regulations, extendable) are quoted because readings (2) and (6) rely on them. The Executive Regulations are not known to have been issued, so the regularisation period cannot be dated: a NAMED GAP, see the Executive Regulations (Q12).

    **Consequence for a user:** The order gives no transitional answer for the regularisation period: its answers on or after 2 January 2022 do not say whether the Art 29 period has run.
