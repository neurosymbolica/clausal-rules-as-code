# eu/dora_ict_third_party_contract_provisions

> **Disclaimer.** These rules are drafted readings derived from the cited statutes. They were generated and tested by automated processes. They are not legal advice and have not been validated by a lawyer. Where a domain lists open questions or alternative readings, they are listed in that domain. The statute text prevails: the code is subsidiary to the statutes.

## Scope

The contract side of the management of ICT third-party risk under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA): for ONE contractual arrangement on the use of ICT services between a financial entity and an ICT third-party service provider, which elements the contract must contain (contract_provisions_required), whether it contains all of them (contract_provisions_sufficient) and which it lacks (contract_provisions_missing), under Art 28(7)(a) to (d) (termination circumstances), Art 30(1) (one written document), Art 30(2)(a) to (i) (elements of every arrangement) and Art 30(3)(a) to (f) (additional elements for ICT services supporting critical or important functions, point (e) split in two, with the microenterprise derogation from point (e) in the closing words of Art 30(3)). Every element, and whether the arrangement supports a critical or important function, is the caller's judgement (true, false or unsure); an unsure judgement that can change the answer is referred to a lawyer. Out of scope: whether the entity is a financial entity within scope and the arrangement one on the use of ICT services (presupposed, Articles 2 and 3), the general principles, strategy, register of information, ...

## Where the open questions are

- `manifest.yaml`: `deferred_set` and `excluded_set` list the provisions deliberately not implemented, with the reason and the consequence for a user; `operative_readings` (where present) records readings of ambiguous text.
- `SIGNATURE.md`: the inputs the domain expects, the answers it gives, and the readings it takes.
- Answers that turn on an unsettled reading are returned as a referral for legal review rather than as a definite answer.
