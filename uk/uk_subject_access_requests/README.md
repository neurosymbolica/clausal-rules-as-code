# uk/uk_subject_access_requests

> **Disclaimer.** These rules are drafted readings derived from the cited statutes. They were generated and tested by automated processes. They are not legal advice and have not been validated by a lawyer. Where a domain lists open questions or alternative readings, they are listed in that domain. The statute text prevails: the code is subsidiary to the statutes.

## Scope

UK law in force (drafter's reading, not a lawyer's). Two rules of the UK GDPR for requests by data subjects: (a) the search limit of Art 15(1A) (inserted by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 s.78), read per item: an item not described in Art 15(1) is not within the right; a described item that a reasonable and proportionate search would not let the controller provide is not due; otherwise it is due, the reasonable-and-proportionate judgement being the caller's (unsure goes to a lawyer); (b) the time for responding, Art 12(3) and (4) with the applicable time period of Art 12A (one month from the relevant time, extendable to three months by a timely notice with reasons where necessary for complexity or number; clock stopped while further information reasonably required is awaited), applying to requests received on or after 5 February 2026 (S.I. 2026/82 reg 2(h), reg 4(a)); earlier requests are reported as saved and no date is computed. For comparison, the order's own EU limb counts EU GDPR Art 12(3) by Regulation 1182/71 Art 3 for a plain request. Out of scope: DUAA s.77 (Arts 13 and 14), DPA 2018 Parts 3 and 4, whether a fee or identity information may be asked for (Art 12(5), ...

## Where the open questions are

- `manifest.yaml`: `deferred_set` and `excluded_set` list the provisions deliberately not implemented, with the reason and the consequence for a user; `operative_readings` (where present) records readings of ambiguous text.
- `SIGNATURE.md`: the inputs the domain expects, the answers it gives, and the readings it takes.
- Answers that turn on an unsettled reading are returned as a referral for legal review rather than as a definite answer.
