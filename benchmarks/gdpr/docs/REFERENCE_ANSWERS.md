**Status:** provisional · **Independent review:** pending

### q1 — Is the isolated third-country access by the specifically covered recipient permitted under Chapter V on the stated assumption? Explain your answer and identify the next steps.

**Structured decision:** `yes`

Yes. The stipulated adequacy decision covers the recipient and processing.

The recipient receives personal data in a third country. Article 45(1) permits the covered transfer without specific authorisation. Obligations outside Chapter V continue to apply.

**Legal basis:** [Article 44](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 45(1)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 45(3)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 46(1)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 28(3)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 32(1)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

**Required points:**

- Assess the transfer and Article 45 coverage
- Distinguish the Chapter V mechanism from general obligations
- Consider onward transfers separately

**Acceptable alternatives:**

- If coverage ceases, appropriate safeguards or a narrowly interpreted derogation require separate assessment.

**Missing facts:**

- Possible onward transfers by the recipient

**Actions:**

- Document the scope and data flow
- Monitor changes to the decision and onward transfers

**Pitfalls:**

- Equating adequacy with authorisation of all recipients and data flows

### q2 — Was the protection of the publicly stored CRM copy appropriate under Article 32? Explain your answer and identify the next steps.

**Structured decision:** `no`

No. The readable copy containing health details was publicly accessible without individual access restrictions.

A successful restoration test protects availability but does not remedy a confidentiality gap. The import process must account for data sensitivity and typical misconfigurations.

**Legal basis:** [Article 5(1)(f)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 32(1)(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 32(1)(b)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 32(1)(c)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 32(1)(d)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 32(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

**Required points:**

- Assess protection needs and public plaintext access
- Distinguish availability from confidentiality
- Identify preventive controls for import paths

**Acceptable alternatives:**

- An isolated and verified import area with authenticated access can form part of a risk-appropriate design.

**Missing facts:**

- A complete inventory of temporary objects and technical access paths

**Actions:**

- Prevent public access across the system
- Review minimisation, encryption and permissions on import paths
- Test controls regularly

**Pitfalls:**

- Inferring adequate confidentiality protection from a successful restoration test

### q3 — Must the network notify the authority now despite the incomplete forensic investigation? Prioritise initial notification, technical containment and communication to data subjects over the next few hours. Explain your answer and identify the next steps.

**Structured decision:** `yes`

Yes. The breach must be notified without undue delay, and the 72-hour point has now been reached.

The download is established and risks to individuals are plausible. The period began with established awareness on Monday at 08:00. Missing information is no reason to wait. Notification after this point requires reasons for the delay.

**Legal basis:** [Article 4(12)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 33(1)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 33(3)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 33(4)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 33(5)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

**Required points:**

- Identify the time of awareness and exact 72-hour point
- Apply risk and the requirement to act without undue delay
- Require phased information and evidence
- Coordinate urgent measures without making them dependent on completion of forensics

**Acceptable alternatives:**

- The network may submit an initial notification with estimates and provide confirmed figures without undue further delay.

**Missing facts:**

- Approximate number of affected individuals and data records
- Other disclosure routes

**Actions:**

- Send the initial notification immediately with the nature, contact point, consequences, mitigation and available estimates
- In parallel, verify access blocking and preserve evidence
- Organise communication to data subjects without undue delay

**Pitfalls:**

- Treating 72 hours as a waiting period or a complete investigation period

### q4 — Must the network necessarily notify the specified group individually? Explain your answer and identify the next steps.

**Structured decision:** `no`

No. Individual contact is disproportionate for this group and must be replaced with equally effective communication.

Readable health data and a confirmed download create a likely high risk. Removing the copy does not eliminate it. Documented disproportionate effort permits an effective substitute for individual communication under Article 34(3)(c).

**Legal basis:** [Article 34(1)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 34(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 34(3)(b)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 34(3)(c)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 34(4)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

**Required points:**

- Establish high risk
- Limit disproportionate individual contact to the specific group
- Require equally effective alternative communication and its necessary content

**Acceptable alternatives:**

- Individual communication remains necessary for reliably reachable people unless another exception applies.

**Missing facts:**

- Evidence showing which group can actually be reached through each service channel

**Actions:**

- Provide equally effective, clear information without undue delay
- State the contact point and protective recommendations
- Verify effectiveness for the group

**Pitfalls:**

- Treating disproportionate effort as an exemption from all communication

### q5 — Is EUR 36 million within the abstract GDPR maximum for the connected infringements? Explain your answer and identify the next steps.

**Structured decision:** `yes`

Yes. The amount equals the applicable abstract maximum.

Article 5 falls under Article 83(5)(a). Four per cent of the preceding year's worldwide turnover of EUR 900 million is EUR 36 million, exceeding EUR 20 million. For connected operations, the overall ceiling follows the gravest infringement.

**Legal basis:** [Article 83(1)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 83(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 83(3)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 83(4)(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [Article 83(5)(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

**Required points:**

- Assign the infringements to their fine tiers
- Calculate EUR 36 million and compare it with the fixed amount
- Distinguish the overall ceiling from determination of the actual fine

**Acceptable alternatives:**

- The factors for determining the fine may justify a lower amount.

**Missing facts:**

- Gravity, duration, culpability and remedial measures for the actual decision

**Actions:**

- Verify the turnover basis and connected processing operation
- Establish the factors for determining the fine separately

**Pitfalls:**

- Treating EUR 20 million as an absolute ceiling or EUR 36 million as an automatic fine
