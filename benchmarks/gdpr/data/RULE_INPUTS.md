# Inputs to the Python rules — release 2.0

The rules answer the yes/no proposition in each question. They receive manually normalised facts as a JSON object. Every key for the rule type is present. `null` denotes an unknown fact. Boolean fields distinguish known `false` from unknown `null`. Risk, necessity and alternatives are testable case assumptions. The reference rubric requires answers to justify them from the narrative and, where needed, seek further facts. This is a limited diagnostic baseline; it is not an independently validated legal decision system.

| `territorial_scope` | The GDPR applies territorially. | `eu_establishment_context`, `offers_to_people_in_eu`, `monitors_behaviour_in_eu`: boolean | Any of the three connecting factors suffices. Offerings concern people in the Union; monitoring concerns behaviour there. Article 3(3) is outside these inputs. |
| `contract_basis` | Article 6(1)(b) supports this processing. | `person_is_contract_party`, `requested_precontractual_steps`, `processing_needed_for_purpose`: boolean | Necessity for a contract with the person or pre-contractual steps at their request. |
| `legitimate_interest` | Article 6(1)(f) supports the specified processing. | `interest`: text; `equally_effective_less_intrusive_option`, `safeguards_in_place`, `reasonable_expectation`, `severe_individual_impact`: boolean | Case-specific balancing for private controllers. An equally effective, less intrusive option defeats the stated processing's necessity. Unresolved balancing facts require a conditional answer. |
| `special_category_processing` | The specified special-category processing is permitted on the stated circumstances. | `data_category`: text; `article_six_basis_identified`, `explicit_consent`, `service_depends_on_consent`, `consent_processing_needed_for_service`, `care_purpose`, `health_law_or_professional_contract`, `professional_secrecy`: boolean | A valid Article 6 basis is required alongside an Article 9(2) exception. The model covers explicit consent and care under Article 9(2)(h) and (3). Apart from the stated conditionality, the scenario stipulates the other circumstances of the explicit-consent declaration. |
| `consent_validity` | The described process provides valid consent. | `affirmative_action`, `purpose_specific`, `informed`, `service_depends_on_consent`, `consent_processing_needed_for_service`: boolean | Affirmative action, specific purpose, information and freely given consent. The dataset supplies no special circumstances that could establish freely given consent despite conditioning service on optional processing. |
| `consent_withdrawal` | Processing for this purpose must end after withdrawal. | `withdrawal_received`, `consent_is_only_basis`: boolean | Applies to the affected purpose following withdrawal where consent is the only basis. Separate purposes are addressed in the rubric. |
| `rights_deadline` | The later response date is covered by the one-month deadline rules. | `received_date`, `extension_notice_date`, `proposed_response_date`: ISO date string; `complex_or_numerous`, `notice_explains_delay`: boolean | One calendar month from receipt. At most two further months where necessary, with notice and reasons within the first month. Cases use weekdays without disputes about holiday rules. |
| `erasure` | The controller must erase the specifically identified data. | `purpose_ended`, `consent_withdrawn_without_other_basis`, `statutory_retention`, `needed_for_legal_claims`: boolean | Article 17(1)(a)/(b) and (3)(b)/(e). Each specified dataset must be assessed independently. |
| `portability` | The specified data fall within the right to data portability. | `provided_by_person`, `automated_processing`: boolean; `basis`: `consent`, `contract`, `legitimate_interest` or `legal_obligation` | Article 20(1). Cases use actively provided data or purely internally derived assessments. Third-party rights and technical feasibility of direct transmission belong in the rubric. |
| `direct_marketing_objection` | Processing for the specified direct-marketing purpose must end after an objection. | `direct_marketing`, `objection_received`: boolean | Article 21(2)/(3), including profiling for that direct marketing. |
| `joint_control` | The parties are joint controllers for the named operation. | `decide_purpose_together`, `decide_essential_means_together`: boolean | Joint decisions for this specific processing stage determine the role. |
| `subprocessor_engagement` | The processor may engage the specified additional provider as planned. | `written_authorization`: `specific`, `general` or `none`; `change_notice_sent`, `objection_opportunity`, `equivalent_data_protection_obligations`, `controller_objected`: boolean | Article 28(2)/(4). General authorisation requires timely notice of changes and an opportunity to object. In these cases, an express objection must be resolved before engagement. |
| `security_controls` | The described protection is appropriate for the specified operation. | `public_access`, `readable_sensitive_data`, `individual_access_controls`, `tested_restore`, `sensitive_data_encrypted`, `keys_separate`: boolean | Article 32 is risk-based. `readable_sensitive_data` means sensitive data readable by unauthorised persons. Open access to such data or absent individual access controls for sensitive data establish a clear deficiency in these cases. Unknown risk-relevant information makes the overall assessment conditional. |
| `breach_authority` | The controller must notify the supervisory authority of the breach. | `personal_data_breach`: boolean; `risk_assessment`: `unlikely`, `risk` or `high`; `hours_since_awareness`: number | Article 33(1)/(3)–(5). A plausible risk to individuals suffices. Hours concern urgency and reasons for delay, not the notification duty itself. |
| `breach_communication` | The controller must notify the data subjects directly. | `personal_data_breach`: boolean; `risk_assessment`: `unlikely`, `risk` or `high`; `data_unintelligible`, `later_measures_avert_high_risk`, `individual_contact_disproportionate`: boolean | Article 34(1)–(3). High risk and no exception. Where individual contact would involve disproportionate effort, equally effective public communication replaces it. |
| `impact_assessment` | A data protection impact assessment is required before operation. | `systematic_extensive_evaluation`, `automated_basis`, `significant_decisions`, `special_or_criminal_data`, `large_scale`, `systematic_public_monitoring`, `other_high_risk_indicators`: boolean | Article 35(1), (3). Paragraph 3(a) requires the first three features; (b) requires large-scale special-category or criminal-offence data; (c) requires large-scale systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas. Unresolved indicators may require further assessment. |
| `data_protection_officer` | A data protection officer must be designated under Article 37(1). | `public_authority`, `court_judicial_capacity`, `monitoring_core_activity`, `regular_systematic_monitoring`, `large_scale_monitoring`, `special_data_core_activity`, `large_scale_special_or_criminal_data`: boolean | The alternatives in Article 37(1). Additional national requirements are outside the benchmark. |
| `international_transfer` | The planned third-country access is currently permitted under Chapter V. | `separate_non_eea_recipient`, `recipient_has_data_access`, `adequacy_covers_recipient`, `safeguards_in_place`, `effective_protection_assessed`, `derogation_applies`: boolean | Access by a separate third-country recipient needs a valid Article 45 or 46 basis, or exceptionally Article 49. Here Article 46 requires appropriate safeguards and established effective protection. |
| `automated_decision` | The prohibition in Article 22(1) covers the described process. | `meaningful_human_review_before_effect`, `legal_or_similarly_significant_effect`, `necessary_for_contract`, `authorized_by_law`, `explicit_consent`, `safeguards_in_place`, `uses_special_categories`, `special_category_exception_applies`: boolean | A solely automated decision with legal or similarly significant effects. Article 22(2)–(4) addresses exceptions and safeguards. |
| `fine_cap` | The amount falls within the applicable abstract maximum. | `infringed_articles`: list of article numbers; `worldwide_prior_year_turnover_eur`, `proposed_fine_eur`: integer EUR amounts | For covered infringements, EUR 10 million or 2% under paragraph 4, or EUR 20 million or 4% under paragraph 5, whichever is higher. The abstract ceiling does not determine the actual fine. |

`unlikely`, `risk` and `high` encode the contextual risk assessment described in the narrative. Article 33 concerns overall risk. For Article 34, the input describes risk before the three separately modelled exceptions—for example, the otherwise high disclosure risk of readable medical findings after a theft. An unresolved risk assessment is `null`. Some rules simplify the legal assessment to the portions listed above. Reference answers and required points remain separate.

The cases and references declare `language: en` and `release: 2.0` in provenance. Stable rule names, field names, enum values and numeric/boolean/null facts remain unchanged. Four free-text interest values have been translated. Per-question `evaluation_tags` are prespecified analysis metadata documented in [CASES.md](../docs/CASES.md); they are not facts supplied to a rule or answer hints for a model. All independent legal and translation review remains pending.
