# ch/swiss_fadp_s2_information_duties_and_dpia

> **Disclaimer.** These rules are drafted readings derived from the cited statutes. They were generated and tested by automated processes. They are not legal advice and have not been validated by a lawyer. Where a domain lists open questions or alternative readings, they are listed in that domain. The statute text prevails: the code is subsidiary to the statutes.

## Scope

Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (DSG, SR 235.1, German text, fedlex consolidation Stand 7 July 2025) Art 19 paras 1 to 4 (the controller's duty to inform the data subject on the collection of personal data and the minimum items it must tell), Art 20 (exceptions to that duty, and the controller's permission to restrict, defer or dispense with the information), Art 21 (the duty to inform of an automated individual decision, the rights to state a point of view and to human review, and the marking of such a decision by a federal body) and Art 22 paras 1, 2, 4 and 5 (whether a data protection impact assessment is required, exempted or may be omitted). For comparison it carries its own readings of EU GDPR Arts 13(1), (2)(f), (4), 14(1), (2)(g), (5), 22(1) and 35(1), (3), (10) and sets three pairs of answers side by side on the same facts. Open-textured terms are judgements of the caller (true, false or unsure); an unsure judgement that decides is referred to a lawyer. ## Where the open questions are

- `manifest.yaml`: `deferred_set` and `excluded_set` list the provisions deliberately not implemented, with the reason and the consequence for a user; `operative_readings` (where present) records readings of ambiguous text.
- `SIGNATURE.md`: the inputs the domain expects, the answers it gives, and the readings it takes.
- Answers that turn on an unsettled reading are returned as a referral for legal review rather than as a definite answer.
